# Modern Electric Tattoo Company — Public Web Research Dossier

**Research captured:** 25 October 2026 (UTC)  
**Location:** Bakersfield, California  
**Scope:** Public business information only. This is a research and website-content brief, not a legal, licensing, financial, or private-person investigation.

> **Important:** Tattoo-shop listings, social profiles, hours, prices, artist rosters, review counts, and service availability change frequently. The strongest finding from this research is that several public sources disagree. Treat the verification list in [Section 12](#12-owner-verification-list-before-publishing) as a required pre-launch checklist.

## 1. Executive summary

Modern Electric Tattoo Company (often shortened to **Modern Electric Tattoo Co.** or **METCO**) is a long-running tattoo studio in Downtown Bakersfield at **1518 18th Street, Bakersfield, CA 93301**, phone **(661) 395-0639**. Its official legacy site says the shop opened in 2011 under veteran tattooer **Billy Von**, who says he has tattooed professionally since 1992 and previously owned Mad Dog Dermagraphics in 1994. Current social posts describe the business as tattooing in Bakersfield since 1992 and mark 15 years at the 18th Street location.

The public brand is built around:

- custom tattooing and traditional/flash work;
- walk-ins plus appointments, subject to artist availability;
- a large, clean, professional, low-drama shop identity;
- a recognizable neon blue panther/cat logo with hot pink, yellow, purple, and black;
- a multi-artist model with both resident and guest artists;
- a strong Downtown Bakersfield/local-community presence;
- periodic flash events, including Friday-the-13th promotions;
- body piercing, although its current availability is not consistently represented online.

The public footprint is substantial but fragmented: an older Wix site remains the most detailed first-party source; Instagram and Facebook are active; Threads and TikTok expose current pricing/promotional language; Yelp, Google-derived directories, Fresha, Nextdoor, MapQuest, Foursquare, and local media provide additional discovery and review signals. The current static site in this repository is more current in design and SEO than the legacy site, but it currently publishes several claims that need reconciliation with the live social footprint.

## 3. History and ownership narrative

### What the first-party story says

The official legacy About/FAQs page says that Modern Electric Tattoo Company opened in 2011 by veteran tattooer Billy Von. It says Billy has tattooed since 1992, was the original owner of **Mad Dog Dermagraphics** when it was above Guthrie's Alley Cat Bar in 1994, traveled throughout the United States applying his craft, and has appeared in tattoo publications, television, and film. Billy's own page repeats that he has been a professional tattooer since 1992, came home to Bakersfield, and opened Modern Electric in 2011.

The site does not name the publications, television programs, or films. Searches found the claim repeated by local-directory/editorial pages, but did not locate a reliable independent list of those appearances. Do not add specific media credits unless the owner supplies them.

### Current longevity signals

Current social indexing is consistent with the long-running narrative:

- Facebook describes the business as being in professional tattooing for over 34 years in Downtown Bakersfield.
- Threads posts refer to 15 years at Modern Electric Tattoo Company and thousands of tattoos at 1518 18th Street.
- The shop's 2011 opening claim means the business reaches its 15-year mark in 2026; the repository's “14+ years” copy is now stale if the date is October 2026.

### Important distinction

“Tattooing since 1992” refers to the founder's professional history. “Modern Electric opened in 2011” refers to the shop. Marketing should keep those statements separate so the business is not accidentally represented as operating under the Modern Electric name since 1992.

## 4. Services and offers found online

### Strongly supported

1. **Custom tattoos.** The official site describes artist consultations and custom work; Yelp and directory descriptions use “custom tattoos” and “body modifications.”
2. **Walk-in tattoos and flash.** Current TikTok and Threads posts repeatedly promote walk-ins, pre-drawn designs, and first-come/first-served availability.
3. **Appointments and consultations.** The legacy site asks clients to select an artist, discuss the idea, then schedule with a deposit.
4. **Cover-ups / reworks.** Public review snippets and the current repository site promote cover-ups; ThreeBestRated reproduces a cover-up testimonial. Confirm which current artists accept cover-ups before presenting it as a shop-wide specialty.
5. **Body piercing.** The legacy site has piercer pages and a piercing booking link; Yelp and Fresha list piercing services; recent social indexing also shows a body-piercer hiring opportunity. Current social posts simultaneously include a “No piercing” message, so availability is in flux.
6. **Guest artists.** The legacy site has a Guest Artists page and names Jack Moore and Danny the Machine as recurring guests. Guest schedules are announced through social media.
7. **Metco T-shirts.** The official legacy home page says full-color front-and-back Metco shirts were sold in-shop for $25. This is an archived price, not a current merchandise guarantee.
8. **Get What You Get machine.** The official legacy page describes a machine that dispenses traditional designs such as eagles, skulls, daggers, angels, hearts, and roses.

### Claims that should not be hard-coded until reconfirmed

- gift certificates;
- complimentary touch-ups;
- tattoo-removal services;
- exact piercing menu and jewelry materials;
- exact current number of resident artists;
- “free consultations” as a universal policy;
- “one of the largest” or “premier” as objective claims;
- commission/booth-rent structure;
- any guarantee of same-day service.

These appear in the repository or third-party descriptions, but they were not all confirmed by a current first-party source.

## 6. Booking, deposits, age, preparation, and shop policies

The following policies come from the official legacy About/FAQs and home/contact pages. They are useful, but should be reconfirmed before being presented as current legal/operational policy.

### Appointment flow

1. Select an artist based on portfolio/style.
2. Discuss the design idea and questions by phone, email, or in person.
3. Consultations are used to assess the work; the shop says it does not quote tattoos online or over the phone.
4. A deposit is required when the appointment is made and is applied to the tattoo total.
5. Arrive on time or early with paperwork, identification, payment, and enough time to complete the design.

### Deposit / rescheduling language

- The legacy FAQ says contact the artist 24 hours in advance to reschedule without losing the deposit.
- A no-show forfeits the deposit.
- The exact deposit amount, refundability, late-arrival grace period, and design-use policy are not published consistently and should be confirmed.

### Preparation guidance

The legacy FAQ recommends being rested, hydrated, and recently fed; showering; charging a phone/tablet; bringing no more than one polite guest; and not bringing children into procedure areas. It also says to wear comfortable, loose clothing that gives access to the area and can be protected from ink.

### Age information

The legacy FAQ attributes age rules to the Kern County Health Department and says:

- tattoos require age 18 and proper identification;
- earlobe piercings under 12 may be considered at the piercer's discretion;
- at 15 and older, many piercings may be available with parental consent and both IDs;
- genital piercings are 18+.

Because age and consent rules are regulated and can change, the website should say “subject to current Kern County requirements; call before booking” unless the shop provides an updated policy.

### Payment information

The official legacy FAQ says major credit cards are accepted, with cash preferred. A current social post says digital and analog payments are accepted. An old Yellow Pages record says cash only. This is a clear directory conflict: publish only what the shop confirms now.

## 8. Brand and voice analysis

### Visual identity

The repository's logo asset is a bold, neon, retro tattoo-shop badge: yellow/orange “MODERN ELECTRIC” lettering, magenta/pink outlines and lightning, a cyan/blue panther or cat, dark black background, and a large “TATTOO” wordmark. The asset supports the existing repository palette of electric blue, neon purple, shock pink, vintage gold, deep black, tattoo cream, and graphite.

The strongest visual direction is **classic American tattoo culture with a bright electric/retro sign treatment**, not a generic “cyberpunk” brand. Use the logo's actual colors and silhouette rather than inventing a new mascot name or changing the mark.

### Repeated public voice

- “Come in as a stranger, leave as a friend.”
- “Experience the experience.”
- “Walk in. Sit down.”
- “Professional tattooing since 1992.”
- “Clean,” “classic,” “professional,” and “no drama.”
- Direct, informal, urgency-based walk-in promotion.
- Community and longevity language: Downtown Bakersfield, 15 years at the location, thousands of tattoos.
- Event-driven offers: Friday the 13th, special flash, pre-drawn designs, and “Get What You Get.”

### Positioning

The public footprint positions the shop as an established, accessible, multi-artist Downtown Bakersfield studio for both spontaneous walk-ins and planned custom work. Its strongest differentiators are longevity, a recognizable visual brand, a central location, a broad roster, and a mix of classic/flash culture with custom consultations.

Avoid claiming “best,” “world-class,” “trusted,” or “premier” as fact unless supported by a specific award or review source; they are marketing opinions, not verifiable business facts.

## 10. Reputation and competitive picture

### Review signals

The available public signals are broadly positive but not uniform:

- **Google:** third-party June 2026 indexing reports 4.4/5 from 133 reviews.
- **Yelp:** May 2026 indexing reports 4.0/5 from 41 reviews; an older November 2025 index reported 39 reviews.
- **ThreeBestRated:** reports a 4.8 score and 116/120 inspection points; this is not the same metric as a review average.
- **Facebook:** the search-visible numbers are likes, talking-about, and check-ins rather than a clearly exposed review score.
- **Yelp review themes:** cleanliness, friendly/reassuring piercers, walk-in convenience, artist skill, and comfortable atmosphere recur in highlighted snippets. Historical listings also contain negative feedback; reputation copy should not imply a perfect record.

### Competitive search visibility

Search results repeatedly place or compare Modern Electric with Bakersfield tattoo businesses including Mad Dog Tattoo & Body Piercing, Third Energy Tattoo, Naked Al's, California Tattoo, Pretty in Ink, Bakersfield Tattoo & Piercing, Voodoo Lily, and other local shops. Modern Electric appears in “best/cheap/traditional/cover-up/piercing” discovery queries, but rankings vary by query and should not be stated as a permanent position.

A useful strategic position is not “we are objectively the best.” It is: **an established Downtown Bakersfield shop with a strong local footprint, traditional/flash energy, multiple artists, and walk-in availability when the schedule allows.**

## 11. Audit of the current repository website

### What is working

- Strong, recognizable dark/neon visual system.
- Clear tap-to-call CTAs and mobile action bar.
- Address and phone are repeated consistently across pages.
- Useful internal linking between the home page and service/location pages.
- Local landing pages, sitemap, robots.txt, `llms.txt`, Open Graph tags, and Schema.org markup are already present.
- The home page communicates the shop's practical conversion path: choose an artist, share an idea, confirm details, arrive ready.

### Factual or operational risks to fix

1. **Stale social metrics.** The repository says 1,361 posts, 3,270 followers, and 677 following in its source text; current Instagram indexing showed 1,371 posts, 3,273 followers, and 754 following. Either remove counts or date-stamp them.
2. **Stale longevity wording.** “14+ years” is outdated as of October 2026 if 2011 is the founding year. Use “since 2011,” “15 years in 2026,” or no count.
3. **Roster mismatch.** The repository presents four resident artists while the old official site has a historical roster and current Instagram tags additional accounts. Build the roster only from owner approval.
4. **Piercing contradiction.** The repository says professional piercing is available and makes safety/material claims. Current public social indexing includes both body-piercer hiring and “No piercing.” Replace with “ask about current piercing availability” until confirmed.
5. **Hours are underspecified.** The repository says “opens at 1 PM daily” and JSON-LD has `openingHours: "Mo-Su 13:00"`, which lacks a closing time and is not an adequate current schedule. Public sources show Sunday/Monday 1–6, Tuesday closure in newer posts, later Friday/Saturday hours, and other directory variants.
6. **Price conflict.** The repository uses flash “$100–$150,” while current social posts say $60 pre-drawn, $120 shop minimum, and $120 black-and-grey/$150 color; the legacy official page says $60 minimum. Remove fixed prices unless dated and approved.
7. **Review claims.** The repository shows “4.7/5 on FB, 166+ Reviews,” but the current Facebook search result exposes 3,593 likes, 11 talking about, and 3,817 check-ins—not a verified 4.7 review score.
8. **Unsupported service claims.** Gift certificates, touch-up policy, free consultations, exact piercing menu, and “expert piercer” need first-party confirmation.
9. **Hiring details.** The home page publishes a separate owner/hiring contact and “high call volume.” Verify that the role and contact are still active before exposing them publicly.
10. **Structured data.** Replace the incomplete `openingHours` string with verified `openingHoursSpecification` entries or omit hours. Verify the coordinates; the repository coordinates differ from coordinates surfaced by current map/directory indexing.
11. **First-party social links.** Add Facebook, Threads, and TikTok to `sameAs` only after confirming the exact official accounts. Keep the legacy Wix site as a source, not necessarily as the canonical destination.
12. **External avatar dependency.** Artist photos load from `unavatar.io/instagram/...`. This introduces third-party availability/privacy/performance risk and can show the wrong image. Use owner-supplied image assets or explicit artist profile URLs.
13. **SEO doorway risk.** The location pages are highly templated and include unverified drive times, parking, prices, and availability. Keep only pages that offer genuinely useful, locally specific content; otherwise consolidate them to avoid thin/duplicative landing pages.
14. **Deployability.** The canonical domain could not be fetched during research. Confirm the site is live, that `https://www.modernelectrictattoo.com/` resolves, and that every sitemap URL returns a successful response before relying on SEO markup.

## 13. Recommended next actions

### Immediate: factual cleanup

1. Create a single approved business-facts JSON or spreadsheet and generate the home page, service pages, `llms.txt`, Schema.org, and sitemap from it.
2. Replace fixed hours/prices/artist counts with verified data or cautious “call for current availability” wording.
3. Remove or date-stamp social counts.
4. Resolve the piercing contradiction before advertising piercing as a live service.
5. Verify the branded domain and redirect strategy from the Wix site.

### Conversion improvements

1. Keep prominent Call, Directions, and Instagram CTAs.
2. Add a short booking form only if the shop has a monitored destination; otherwise do not create a form that goes nowhere.
3. Show the booking inputs clients need: artist, concept, reference images, placement, approximate size, color/black-and-grey, timing, and contact method.
4. Create a clearly dated “Current Walk-In / Flash” module controlled by one person.
5. Put the no-online-quote policy near the booking CTA so expectations are clear.

### Local SEO / reputation

1. Claim and maintain the Google Business Profile; confirm the NAP, hours, services, photos, and booking URL.
2. Make Yelp, Facebook, Instagram, Wix, Fresha, MapQuest, Nextdoor, and other major directory NAP data agree.
3. Add fresh shop-interior, exterior, healed-work, artist, and accessibility photos.
4. Ask real clients for honest reviews after appointments and respond professionally to both positive and negative feedback.
5. Use local service pages only when each page has unique useful information—not just a changed neighborhood name.

### Content strategy grounded in the actual footprint

- weekly artist/portfolio feature;
- current flash availability;
- healed work and aftercare education;
- walk-in updates with same-day hours;
- Friday-the-13th/event announcements;
- shop-history and Downtown Bakersfield stories;
- guest-artist announcements;
- piercing updates only when the service is staffed and bookable;
- short video clips that show the shop environment, not only finished tattoos.
